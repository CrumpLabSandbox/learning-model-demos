Learning Model Demos

How do people and animals learn what predicts what? Psychologists answer with models: exact rules that a computer can run. This site lets you run those models one step at a time and see why they behave as they do.

You do not need any background in learning theory, and you do not need to like maths. Start wherever you are comfortable, and go back a level whenever you need to.

Rescorla-Wagner (1972)Learning from surprise. Explains blocking and overshadowing; fails on latent inhibition and negative patterning.SlidesEssentials · Everything
Mackintosh (1975)Attention rises for the best predictor of the outcome, and each cue learns from its own error.SlidesEssentials · Everything
Pearce-Hall (1980)Attention follows recent surprise; cues learn excitatory and inhibitory strength.SlidesEssentials · Everything
SOP (Wagner, 1981)Learning moment by moment, from what is active in memory at the same time as the outcome. Timing inside and between trials matters.SlidesEssentials · Everything
Pearce (configural) (1987)The whole pattern of cues on a trial learns as one unit and lends its strength to similar patterns. Explains negative patterning and one-trial overshadowing.SlidesEssentials · Everything
Delamater (network) (2012)A small neural network: cues reach the outcomes through a hidden layer that learns its own representations, with more than one outcome. Explains acquired equivalence and biconditional discriminations.SlidesEssentials · Everything
MINERVA-AL (Jamieson, Crump, & Hannah, 2012)Learning without associations: store every trial in memory, and expect what similar trials brought. Explains backward blocking and negative patterning.SlidesEssentials · Everything
Judging contingency (people)How people judge what goes with what: the 2 × 2 table and ΔP, streamed trials you can take yourself, outcome density, cue interaction, and where the models stop and the decision begins.SlidesStreamed trials · Tutorial

Which model explains what

Every finding has a page of its own, with the evidence and the experiments behind it, on the phenomena page. Every finding run through every model is one table on the models page. To see why a model does or does not show an effect, open that model with the experiment loaded, or run one experiment through several models side by side on the comparison page.

For instructors

  • Every slide deck has speaker notes: press N in a deck. Press F for full screen and O for a list of slides.
  • Printing a deck gives one slide per page with its notes, for handouts.
  • The Copy link button on a model page saves the experiment, settings, and selected trial, so a link in your slides opens exactly that view.
  • Each model is tested against published results before it is used in class. Source and plan are on GitHub.
