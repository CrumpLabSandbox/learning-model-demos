Tutorial · about 15 minutes

Learning without associations

Every model before MINERVA-AL keeps a strength for each cue and updates it. MINERVA-AL keeps no strengths. It remembers every trial, and when cues appear, it recalls the trials most like now. Learning is remembering.

Events are lists of numbers

To store whole events, MINERVA-AL writes each one as a vector: each stimulus has its own block of features, 1s when it is present and 0s when it is not. Tick the boxes to build an event.

Recalling is adding up similar traces

The cues present, the probe, are compared with every trace in memory. Each trace counts in proportion to its similarity, cubed, and they add up to the echo: what the learner expects. Try the three probes.

Storing only the surprise

Each trial stores what happened minus what memory expected: discrepancy encoding. That one choice produces most of what the other models explain with strengths.

What memory can do that strengths cannot

Negative patterning

AB is most similar to the AB traces, and cubing makes them count far more than the A and B traces. The compound is remembered as its own pattern.

Try it yourself

On the MINERVA-AL page, step to a trial in the second phase and look at the Memory panel: the red features in B's block are the opposite of B. The model and its tests follow Jamieson, Crump, and Hannah (2012).
