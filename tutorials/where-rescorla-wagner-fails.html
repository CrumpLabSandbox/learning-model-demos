Where Rescorla-Wagner fails

Rescorla-Wagner explains a lot with one equation: acquisition, extinction, blocking, overshadowing, and conditioned inhibition. Its failures are just as useful, because each one points to an idea a later model added.

Each failure below comes from one feature of the equation, ΔV = αβ(λ − ΣV). If the equation is new to you, start with the overview slides.

1. Latent inhibition: nothing to learn when nothing happens

A cue shown many times on its own is learned about more slowly later. In Rescorla-Wagner, pre-exposure changes nothing: λ and VA are both 0, so the error is 0, and αA is a fixed parameter.

Fixed by attention that changes (Pearce-Hall) and old traces of the cue alone (MINERVA-AL). In SOP the context comes to call the cue up, so the pre-exposed cue starts slower, but with the model's activity limits on it catches up within a few trials and the effect is too small to count.

2. Backward blocking: absent cues never change

After AB+ training, training A alone weakens B, even though B is never shown again. Rescorla-Wagner only changes the cues that are present, so B keeps exactly what it learned.

Fixed by MINERVA-AL: A brings back B from memory, B is not there, and the new traces store the opposite of B.

3. Negative patterning: a compound is just a sum

Animals learn that A alone and B alone mean the outcome, but A and B together do not. In Rescorla-Wagner the compound predicts VA + VB, so it always predicts more than either part.

Fixed by models that can treat a compound as something other than the sum of its parts: MINERVA-AL, which retrieves the traces most like AB; Pearce, for which AB is its own configuration; and Delamater's network, whose hidden layer learns a pattern for AB.

4. Time: a trial is a single step

A gap between the cue and the outcome weakens learning, the order of cue and outcome matters, and spaced trials teach more than massed ones. Rescorla-Wagner has no time inside a trial or between trials, so none of these can matter.

Fixed by SOP, which runs moment by moment.

What the failures teach

Each failure is a precise prediction that turned out wrong, which is what makes a model useful. The later models each keep part of Rescorla-Wagner's idea and change one thing: what drives attention, what is active when, or what is stored in memory.
