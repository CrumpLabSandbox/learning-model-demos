Tutorial · about 12 minutes

Time inside the trial

For the trial-by-trial models, a trial is one step: these cues, this outcome. Real experiments happen in time. The gap between a cue and the outcome, their order, and the time between trials all change what is learned. SOP is the model on this site that can see them.

The idea in one picture

Each stimulus is a set of elements that are inactive, in A1 (vivid, brief), or in A2 (fading, lingering). A cue gains strength when it and the US are both in A1 at the same moments, and loses strength when it is in A1 while the US is in A2. The notation is explained in Reading the equations, section 16.

A gap between cue and outcome

If the US arrives long after the cue has gone, the cue's elements have already left A1. There is almost no overlap, so almost nothing is learned: trace conditioning is weak.

The outcome first, the cue second

When the cue follows the outcome, it is active while the US is fading in A2. The loss beats the gain by a little on every trial, and over many trials the cue becomes an inhibitor: backward conditioning.

Trials close together

With only a short gap, the last trial is still active when the next begins.

Try it yourself

On the SOP page, open Inside the trial and press Play. Then write your own timing in the design, such as 20 A+ [CS 1-10, US 15-16], and watch the green area shrink as the gap grows.
