Streamed trials: be the participant

In a contingency judgement experiment, people watch a cue and an outcome come and go, then say how strongly the two are related. Here you can do the task yourself, the way it was run in the streamed-trial studies: a whole block of frames in a few seconds, then one judgement. Afterwards you see the answer, and what every model on this site made of the very same frames.

Each frame shows a square (the cue) or not, and a circle (the outcome) or not. Some streams add a triangle as a second cue. New here? The overview slides explain the task first, and the tutorial connects it to the models. The stream flashes; the default speed is gentle, and the options let you choose.

The procedure follows Crump, Hannah, Allan & Hord (2007), Quarterly Journal of Experimental Psychology, 60, 753–761, and Hannah, Crump, Allan & Siegel (2009), Canadian Journal of Experimental Psychology, 63, 103–112. Their frames lasted 100 ms with 100 ms black gaps; the cell counts in the preset streams are theirs.
